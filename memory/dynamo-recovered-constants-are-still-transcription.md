Three evaluated heads of one task, same concept, rising strength: 6 stated subsystems → **3/5 solved**; 8 subsystems with two constants recovered from evidence → **2/2 on pass@2**; 9 subsystems including a cost model behind an unknown hinge (non-linear until swept) → **4/5 solved, avg 0.800**. Every intended crux — per-candidate die walk, exact integer ceiling, three-key plan optimum, loading-order optimum, rational-bracket coefficient recovery, hinge recovery — was solved by every agent. The only failures were reading slips on single definitional sentences, roughly 20% per trial against the 60% the pass@5 band needs.

**Why:** "recover the constant from a log" is not inference when the charter also states *how* to recover it. It is one more transcribed procedure; it adds implementation volume and leaves the failure rate flat. The agents read the charter, wrote the whole planner in one step, fixed one bug, and finished with 13 minutes of the hour spare.

**How to apply:** judge the *shape* before building. A complete normative contract graded on exact outputs caps at 3–4/5 no matter how many interacting rules it carries — see [[dynamo-cosine-change-the-question]] for picking the question. Spend the difficulty budget on a mechanism careful reading cannot supply: irreversibility that punishes a draft run against the graded fixture, or a policy that is genuinely unwritten and must be induced from labelled outcomes. And when a draw returns 2/2 with the analyser noting a one-step implementation and spare budget, treat it as the ceiling reporting itself, not as a draw to re-roll.
